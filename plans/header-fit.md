# Header title shrinks to fit (user call, 2026-11-18)

A long artist name wrapped to many lines and filled, or overflowed, the header (fixed or floored
height), and the character cap that hid it was too tight for real customers. First attempt: let the
header grow downward. It was built for layout 4 only, did nothing for Retro layout 1 (an
`aspectRatio` + `overflow: hidden` root) and was **reverted**. Second, shipped: **the title sets
smaller, gradually, as the name gets longer.**

## Decisions (user, 2026-11-18)

- Floor: **50 %** of the size the layout draws the name at. Below it, never.
- Past the floor: **a character cap** on the Title box (high, up to 80), sized so the floor always
  fits. No growing, no clipping. Where another element prints the name (a card's caption, a seal) its
  own limit still caps the box: see *Caps*.
- One mechanism for every layout and template; the header's height, seals, grain and aspect ratios do not move.

## Mechanism

`titleFitK(theme, design, dev, words, space)` (`data.js`) → `vm.titleK`, the fraction of the layout's
own size (1 for a name that fits, which is every seed: `digest.mjs` before / after over
`0,1,2,3,4 header` is byte-identical). `EncoreSection` does no maths: `fitSz(s, expr, token)` returns
the layout's own expression untouched when `titleK` is 1, else
`max(token × floor, min(expr, token × titleK))`, and holds the floor even at `titleK` 1 where the
layout's own whole-line fit (`100cqi / navNameEms`: Editorial's and Pop's layout 1) would run under it
(the name then wraps at 50 %). The header `Title` takes `overflow-wrap: break-word` while it is fitted.

`fitTitleSize` simulates greedy word wrap in ems (the per-face tables: `bebasEms`, `antonEms`,
`gloockEms`, `titanEms`, and the new `frauncesEms` for Retro, generated by `scripts/ems-table.mjs`)
and bisects for the largest size ≤ base at which `lines × lh × size ≤ H`. A 1 % margin sits on the
width test; a word wider than the column never fits.

`H = min(TITLE_FIT.share (0.4) × header height, the free room at that site)`. `TITLE_SITES`
(`data.js`) holds, per template × design, the base size, line height, wrapping model (`il`), column
width `W`, header height `R`, `room` and, at layout 1, `bs` (the room beside the portrait card). They
were read off long-name renders, not derived. Retro is **not** in `navFace` (its other branches test
whether `navNameEms` is set); `vm.titleK` uses `navFace ?? frauncesEms`.

**Layout 1 has two states.** The portrait card and the title share a wrapping row: a name whose
one-line width fits beside the card stays on one line; otherwise the whole column drops under the card at
the full width and wraps (Retro desktop: ~224 px of room). `fitTitleSize` takes either state (`beside`),
and layout 1 guarantees one line at the base size, every other layout two (`minLines`).

The knob: `TITLE_FIT.share` (how much of the header a long name may take).

## A page narrower than its frame

The desktop canvas card is `width: 100%` of what the sidebar leaves, so a 1440 window draws the page
1088 wide, and layout 1's root (an aspect ratio) is 566 tall there, not 614. The first build fitted for
1180 and clipped the tags in the real editor at 1440 (the harness never showed it). `sectionVm` takes
`pageW`: the editor measures the canvas (`deskW`, a `ResizeObserver` on the scroller, desktop tab only),
`PublishedPage` passes its width on a phone narrower than 390, and the harness takes `&fw=`. `titleFitK`
takes the lost width off every column; at layout 1, whose root is the aspect box at every template, it takes
the lost height off the room as well (the nav, card and tags do not give it back) and keeps 6 % of what
is left. The other layouts' roots grow with their content, so they only lose width. The floor falls
with the page too (0.5 × `pageW` / frame) and is returned with `k`, so `vm.titleFloor` is per page.
Published and Preview at desktop are the 1180 frame and pass nothing.

**Proof is the real editor, not the harness**: `real3.mjs` in the session scratch (select the Title box
or, with `INLINE=1`, the canvas h1; type the 82-character string; assert no clipping element holds the
h1, the h1 is inside its root and no tag chip meets it), run over five templates × layouts 1–4 at 1440
and at 1250.

**Known limit.** At a canvas narrower than about 1000 (a window under ~1350), a name near the 80-character
cap can still clip at layout 1: the fixed nav, card and tags take their full height whatever the width, so
the proportional floor does not fit (Lime and Grunge at 1250; Editorial's 390 page at 360, by 6 px). The
caps are for the frame and the 1088 canvas. The answer, if wanted, is a width-dependent cap or a lower floor.

## Caps

`limits.mjs header --keys=title --sweep [--why]` scans lengths 4…80 in steps of 4 for each design and
width and takes the first failing length less one; `FITTED` names the key (floor 0.5, no line census,
`box` / `overlap` / `page` kept). The title's own lines are measured as **line boxes** (`?linebox=1`,
h1 only): the font's em box runs a quarter of its size past a .75 line.

**What the probe judges.** The fitted name is judged on its own seat, the h1 (`inH1`), as line boxes,
with a 1.5 px tolerance (0.2 em where the layout lifts it: Pop's `-0.14em`) and without overlap against a
seal's svg (its box is 1.38× the disc; a one-pixel brush by a line box is read by eye). The cards that
print the name too (Retro layout 3's polaroid caption, the portrait cards, JP-062) are not seats: their
limit is the cap. Retro layout 3 therefore holds **14** (the polaroid's `nowrap` caption clips the name);
the other layout 3s wrap the name inside the card, seen at 78 characters. With that, `--check` is green;
before it the strict probe failed 7 caps that were all of the kinds above.

## Not done

The cards that print the name by their own rules keep their own limits and are not fitted: Retro
layout 3's polaroid caption (`nowrap`, clipped), the layout-3 portrait cards (JP-062), the seal
rings (`sealCap`). They cap the Title box at the lengths in `data.js`.

## The height allowance

`H = min(0.4 × header height, free room, 2 × lh × base)` (user call, 2026-11-18). The third term is the
two-line cap: a name of two lines stays at the layout's own size, a third line is where it starts to
shrink. Without it, a 1180 canvas (windows of ~1476 and up) let three lines stand at full size, the
0.4 share of layout 1's 614px being 245px, and nothing visibly shrank.
